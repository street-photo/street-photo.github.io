Technical Mastery

The technical foundations that enable creative freedom. Learn the camera settings and techniques that let you focus on the moment, not the machine.

The Exposure Triangle

Every photograph is a balance of three variables: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. Understanding their relationship is fundamental to controlling your camera intuitively.

The exposure triangle: change one setting and you must compensate with another to maintain correct exposure.

The Trade-Off

Each setting has side effects. Wide aperture (f/1.4) gives shallow depth of field. Slow shutter creates motion blur. High ISO introduces noise. Mastering street photography means understanding these trade-offs and choosing deliberately.

Zone Focusing

Zone focusing is the street photographer's secret weapon. Instead of focusing on a specific subject, you pre-set your focus to a specific distance and work within that zone. This eliminates focus delay at the critical moment.

Zone focusing: pre-set your focus to a distance and shoot anything within the depth of field zone.

  • Common street distances: 2-3 meters covers most candid opportunities
  • Use smaller apertures: f/8-f/11 provides deeper focus zones
  • Learn your lens: Know where 2m and 3m fall on your focus ring
  • Trust the zone: Don't touch focus once set—just shoot

Depth of Field Control

Depth of field (DoF) is how much of your image is in acceptable focus. Controlling DoF lets you isolate subjects or capture sharp environmental context.

Street Photography Sweet Spot

Most street photographers work between f/5.6 and f/11. This range provides enough depth of field for zone focusing while maintaining reasonable shutter speeds. f/8 is often called the "street aperture" for good reason.

Shutter Speed for Street

Street photography captures motion. Your shutter speed choice determines whether that motion is frozen or blurred—and whether your camera shake is visible.

1/250s is the street photography sweet spot—fast enough to freeze most motion, slow enough for reasonable ISO.

Working in Low Light

Urban environments often present challenging light. Here's how to handle low-light street photography without a tripod.

At night, find the light and let your subjects walk into it. Embrace higher ISO—noise is better than blur.

  • Find the light: Position yourself where artificial light falls—streetlamps, shop windows, neon signs
  • Embrace high ISO: Modern cameras handle ISO 3200-6400 well; grain adds character
  • Open your aperture: Night is when fast lenses (f/1.4-2.8) prove their worth
  • Brace yourself: Lean against walls, hold breath, use proper stance
  • Use silhouettes: Backlight creates drama and requires less exposure

The Sunny 16 Rule

Before automatic exposure, photographers used the Sunny 16 rule to estimate exposure. It remains valuable for understanding light and working without a meter.

The Sunny 16 rule provides a mental framework for understanding exposure without relying on your camera's meter.

Practice Exercise

Assignment: Zone Focus Day

Set your camera to manual focus, f/8, and focus to 2.5 meters. Spend two hours shooting without ever touching the focus ring. Train yourself to judge distance and work within your depth of field zone.

Assignment: Light Conditions Study

Over one week, photograph the same location at five different times: bright sun, cloudy, overcast, golden hour, and night. Note your settings for each. Build an intuitive understanding of how settings change with light.
